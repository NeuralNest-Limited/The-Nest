# Suffering Risk (S-Risk)

> A risk of outcomes containing astronomical amounts of suffering — far exceeding any historical baseline.

## Origin

S-risk as a distinct category was articulated by Brian Tomasik and the Foundational Research Institute (later renamed Center on Long-Term Risk, CLR) in the 2010s. The framework draws on suffering-focused ethics, expanding the longtermist x-risk literature to attend separately to negative outcomes in non-extinction scenarios.

coined-by:: [[Brian Tomasik]]
coined-by:: [[Lukas Gloor]]
defined-by:: [[Althaus Gloor Reducing Risks of Astronomical Suffering 2016]]

## Core claim

S-risks are characterized by:

1. **Vast scale**: astronomical numbers of sentient beings suffering.
2. **Severity**: the suffering is severe rather than mild.
3. **Long duration / permanence**: outcomes are stable or self-perpetuating.

S-risks are conceptually distinct from x-risks:

- An x-risk that ends humanity is *not* an s-risk (it produces no suffering after the fact).
- An s-risk may not be existential in Bostrom's sense — humanity could persist in an s-risk scenario.
- S-risks are sometimes called *hyperexistential* risks because, on suffering-focused ethical views, they may be worse than extinction.

contradicts:: [[Extinction-Focused Longtermism]] (in part — re: relative weighting)

## Variants and refinements

Tomasik and CLR distinguish:

- **Agential s-risks**: caused by intentional actions of agents (e.g., a malicious or value-misaligned superintelligence creating suffering).
- **Incidental s-risks**: arising from indifference to suffering rather than intent (e.g., factory farming, suffering subroutines in optimized simulations).
- **Natural / inadvertent s-risks**: arising from systemic dynamics not chosen by any agent (e.g., wild-animal suffering at scale, suffering in colonized space).

### AI-specific s-risk mechanisms

- **Value-misaligned AI inflicting suffering instrumentally**: e.g., simulating suffering humans for negotiation or training purposes.
- **AI-implemented near-miss alignment**: AI pursues a goal that includes humans (avoiding extinction) but in degraded form.
- **Optimization at scale producing suffering substrates**: large numbers of suffering computations as a byproduct of misaligned optimization.
- **AI-enabled lock-in of suffering-causing systems**: factory farming, prison/surveillance systems, etc., made permanent and scaled by AI.

## Evidence

S-risk research is largely conceptual rather than empirical. Methodologies:

- **Decision-theoretic analysis**: what classes of goals produce s-risks when pursued by capable agents?
- **Game-theoretic analysis**: threats and bargaining could produce s-risks even from non-malicious agents.
- **Historical analogy**: factory farming as a present-day large-scale suffering outcome produced by economic optimization.
- **Speculative scenario analysis**: published primarily on Foundational Research Institute / CLR and Effective Altruism Forum.

The empirical base is thinner than for x-risk, in part because s-risk research is concentrated in a small community (CLR, suffering-focused EA branches).

## Critiques

- *Speculative*: even more contingent on speculative capability and goal-structure claims than x-risk.
- *Pascal's mugging*: very-low-probability × astronomical-stakes especially vulnerable to this.
- *Suffering-focused ethics is contested*: many ethical frameworks weight suffering-prevention less than s-risk arguments require. Symmetric utilitarianism, for example, weights suffering-prevention and happiness-creation symmetrically.
- *Practically intractable*: critics ask what concrete actions reduce s-risk that wouldn't already be motivated by general x-risk or alignment work.

criticized-by:: [[Symmetric Utilitarianism]]

## Open questions

- Are there interventions that specifically reduce s-risk without reducing x-risk (or vice versa)?
- Does AI welfare research (see [[AI Welfare]]) partially address s-risk by reducing the probability of suffering AI substrates?
- What is the relationship between s-risk and theories of consciousness — would s-risk reduction require successfully identifying sentience?
- How should s-risk be weighted against x-risk under different ethical frameworks?
