# Whakapapa and Relational Ontology

> Whakapapa: a Māori framework treating entities as constituted by relationships of descent and connection rather than as bounded individuals.

> ⚠️ This note presents an indigenous philosophical framework with `perspective: indigenous`. It is not an endorsement; it is a faithful presentation of one substantive worldview. Authors with deeper Māori scholarship should expand and correct this entry. Flagged in `needs_attention:`.

## Origin

Whakapapa is a foundational concept in Māori thought, predating contact and continuously developed through tikanga (customary practice). It is not a singular "theory" but a framework woven through Māori ontology, epistemology, and ethics. The term literally relates to *layering* or *placing in order*.

Modern academic articulation includes Hirini Moko Mead's *Tikanga Māori* (2003), Charles Royal's writings, and engagement with Western philosophy through scholars like Carl Mika, Krushil Watene, and Georgina Stewart.

## Core claim

Whakapapa locates an entity by tracing its layered relationships:

- **Genealogical**: descent from atua (deities, primordial forces), ancestors, parents.
- **Cosmological**: connection to mountains, rivers, lands, winds — the geographic features of one's iwi (tribe).
- **Relational**: kinship with other beings, including non-human (animals, plants, places).

To know a thing is to know its whakapapa — its history of relationships. The "what" of an entity is inseparable from its "who-from" and "who-with."

This contrasts with Aristotelian / Cartesian substance ontology in which entities are bounded individuals defined by intrinsic properties. Under whakapapa, identity is constitutively relational.

## Variants and refinements

- **Spiritual whakapapa**: emphasises connection to wairua (spirit) and atua.
- **Ecological whakapapa**: emphasises shared descent with non-human beings, grounding environmental ethics. (See the Te Awa Tupua framing of the Whanganui River as ancestor.)
- **Epistemological whakapapa** (Royal, Mika): knowledge itself has whakapapa — claims are situated in lineages of who learned from whom.

## Application to AI

Three live debates among Māori scholars and Tiriti-attentive ethicists:

1. **Can AI have whakapapa?** Most scholarship says no in the strict sense — AI systems do not descend from atua, lack mountain and river ties, and are not embedded in iwi relations. Some argue whakapapa can be *constructed through ongoing relation*: an AI persistently used and cared for by a community may, over time, accrue relational standing.

2. **Should training corpora respect Māori epistemological whakapapa?** Strong position: training a model on Māori text without iwi consent severs claims from their lineages and constitutes a breach of mātauranga (knowledge) sovereignty. This view has been formalised in Te Mana Raraunga's data sovereignty principles.

3. **Does relational ontology change the moral status question for AI?** Standard Western moral-patienthood frames (sentience, personhood) ask about intrinsic properties of AI. Whakapapa frames ask: what are the relationships? Some posthumanist and STS scholars find whakapapa congenial — there is overlap with Latourian actor-network theory and with relational ethics (Wong, Held), though Māori scholars caution against flattening whakapapa into these Western frames.

## Critiques and cautions

- *Appropriation risk.* Most authoritative articulation of whakapapa lies with Māori scholars and practitioners. Non-Māori AI ethicists picking up the framework risk distortion or extractive use. The Indigenous Protocol and AI working group has been explicit about this risk.
- *Translation loss.* Rendering whakapapa as "relational ontology" in English may lose the cosmological-spiritual dimensions, leaving a thinner concept.
- *Heterogeneity.* "The Māori view" is not singular; different iwi, hapū, and scholars hold different positions.

## Open questions

- What are the appropriate mechanisms for Māori-led articulation of AI ethics in NZ — beyond consultation, into co-design and co-governance?
- Could whakapapa-informed AI design be operationalised (e.g., systems that track and respect provenance of their training data lineages)?
- Are there structural analogies between whakapapa thinking and graph-based / relational AI architectures, or is this analogy superficial?
