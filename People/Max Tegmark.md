# Max Tegmark

> MIT cosmologist who became one of the most prominent AI x-risk advocates through his founding of the Future of Life Institute (2014) and his book *Life 3.0* (2017). Central organizer of the March 2023 "Pause Giant AI Experiments" open letter.

## Background

Born 1967 in Sweden. PhD in physics from UC Berkeley (1994). MIT faculty since 2004, currently Professor in the Department of Physics. Best-known academic work outside AI is on cosmology and the mathematical universe hypothesis (his earlier book *Our Mathematical Universe*).

Founded the Future of Life Institute (FLI) in 2014 with Jaan Tallinn, Anthony Aguirre, Meia Chita-Tegmark, and Viktoriya Krakovna.

## Contributions

### Future of Life Institute (founding and direction)
Founded FLI in 2014 with the mission of reducing existential and large-scale risks from transformative technologies. Under his presidency, FLI has been central to AI x-risk advocacy.

affiliated-with:: [[Future of Life Institute]]

### Life 3.0 (2017)
Book-length treatment of AI futures and existential risk for a general audience. Established his public profile on AI topics.

### 2017 Asilomar AI Conference and Principles
Organized the FLI-hosted Asilomar conference and the resulting AI Principles signed by hundreds of researchers and executives.

cited-in:: [[Future of Life Institute]]
cited-in:: [[Asilomar Recombinant DNA Precedent]] (analogous)

### 2023 Pause Letter
Organized "Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter" calling for a six-month pause on training AI systems more powerful than GPT-4. Signed by Bengio, Musk, Wozniak, Russell, and thousands of others — the most prominent x-risk-advocacy moment in mainstream AI discourse to date.

authored-by:: [[FLI Pause Letter 2023]]

## Positions

`perspective: decel` / `cautious`

Tegmark's public stance:
- AI x-risk is real, substantial, and under-prioritized.
- Voluntary lab restraint is insufficient; coordinated pauses with enforcement are warranted.
- Existing safety research is far short of what frontier capabilities require.

Less radical than Yudkowsky's "Shut It All Down" position; more cautious than mainstream lab framings.

## Reception

- *Within decel / cautious community*: foundational figure for the modern advocacy programme.
- *Within mainstream AI*: mixed — Pause Letter both legitimized x-risk concerns broadly and drew critique for vagueness ("Pause what, exactly?").
- *From accelerationist quarters*: paradigm opponent.
- *Public profile*: high; among the most recognizable AI x-risk advocates.
