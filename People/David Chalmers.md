# David Chalmers

> Philosopher of mind; coiner of the "hard problem of consciousness" (1995); co-director of NYU's Center for Mind, Brain, and Consciousness. Recent work has applied his consciousness frameworks to artificial intelligence, including co-authorship of the influential Butlin et al. (2023) AI consciousness assessment.

## Background

Born 1966 in Sydney. PhD in philosophy and cognitive science from Indiana University (1993) under Douglas Hofstadter. Positions at UCSC, UC Santa Barbara, University of Arizona, ANU, and (since 2009) NYU as University Professor.

## Contributions

### The hard problem of consciousness (1995)
Coined in a 1995 paper and developed in *The Conscious Mind* (1996): the distinction between the "easy" problems of consciousness (explaining cognitive functions like attention, reportability, integration) and the "hard" problem (explaining *why* there is subjective experience at all). The framework has structured consciousness studies for three decades.

coined-by:: [[Hard Problem of Consciousness]]
defined-by:: [[The Conscious Mind 1996]]

### Constructing the World (2012)
Major work in metaphysics and philosophy of language; influential within philosophy though less so in AI discourse.

### Reality+ (2022)
Argues virtual reality experiences can be genuinely real and meaningful, that we may be in a simulation but this would not undermine the reality of our world, and engages questions of virtual / artificial mind. Public-facing entry into AI-adjacent philosophy.

### AI consciousness work (2023+)
Co-authored "Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence" (Butlin, Long, Chalmers et al. 2023), applying current scientific theories of consciousness to AI systems. Has given public talks on whether current or near-future LLMs could be conscious — generally taking a "uncertain but possible" position.

authored-by:: [[Butlin Long Chalmers Consciousness in AI 2023]]

## Positions

`perspective: safety-pragmatist` (re: AI consciousness uncertainty)

Chalmers' position on AI consciousness: scientific uncertainty is genuine; current LLMs probably lack consciousness on most leading theories; near-future systems are uncertain enough to warrant taking the question seriously.

He has expressed sympathy for moral-status precaution (a position closer to the AI welfare community than to skeptics).

## Reception

Foundational figure in philosophy of mind. Reception:

- *Within philosophy*: highly influential; the hard-problem framing structures the field even among those who reject it.
- *Within AI ethics / welfare*: increasingly central as the field professionalizes.
- *From eliminativists / strong physicalists*: critiqued; Daniel Dennett and Patricia Churchland have argued against the hard-problem framing.
- *From scientists of consciousness*: mixed; some (Seth, in part) treat the hard problem as well-posed but answerable; others reject the framing.
