# Geoffrey Hinton

> Cognitive scientist whose work on backpropagation, Boltzmann machines, and deep neural networks is foundational to modern AI. Turing Award (2018) and Nobel Prize in Physics (2024, shared with John Hopfield) recipient. In 2023 resigned from Google to speak publicly about AI risks.

## Background

Born 1947 in the UK; primarily based in Canada since 1987. PhD in artificial intelligence from Edinburgh (1978). Faculty at Carnegie Mellon, then University of Toronto from 1987. Long affiliation with Google Brain (2013–2023), joining after Google acquired his startup DNNresearch. Co-founder of the Vector Institute (Toronto, 2017).

## Contributions

### Foundational deep learning work
- Co-author of the canonical backpropagation paper (Rumelhart, Hinton, Williams 1986).
- Boltzmann machines and Restricted Boltzmann Machines.
- AlexNet (with students Krizhevsky and Sutskever, 2012) — the ImageNet result that catalyzed the deep learning revolution.
- Capsule networks (2017+).
- Distillation (2015) — training small models from large ones.

### Turing Award (2018) and Nobel (2024)
Turing Award shared with Bengio and LeCun. Nobel Prize in Physics 2024 shared with John Hopfield "for foundational discoveries and inventions that enable machine learning with artificial neural networks." The Nobel committee's choice was itself notable — physics rather than chemistry or a putative future "AI Prize."

### Public risk advocacy (2023–present)
- Resigned from Google in May 2023, citing the desire to speak freely about AI risks.
- Subsequent interviews (NYT, BBC, MIT Technology Review) articulated concerns about: loss of control, autonomous-agent risks, AI-amplified disinformation, near-term economic displacement.
- Co-signed CAIS extinction-risk statement and FLI letter (initially declined the latter but later expressed similar concerns).
- Co-authored *Managing AI Risks in an Era of Rapid Progress* (Science, 2024).

## Positions

`perspective: cautious`

Hinton's stated p(doom)-style probabilities have ranged 10–50% across interviews, though he has expressed methodological skepticism of such numbers. His framing emphasises the *novelty* of the risk: humans have no prior experience managing entities potentially more intelligent than themselves.

Hinton has distinguished his concern from "doomerism" — he supports continued development with robust safety investment, not pause.

## Reception

Hinton's resignation from Google was a major media event and accelerated mainstream attention to AI x-risk discussions. Reception parallels Bengio's: welcomed by safety community; critiqued by accelerationists; mixed within mainstream ML.
