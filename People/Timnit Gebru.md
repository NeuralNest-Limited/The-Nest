# Timnit Gebru

> Computer scientist whose work documented bias in commercial AI systems and articulated systemic critiques of large language models. Co-founded Google's Ethical AI team; fired by Google in December 2020 in a public controversy that catalyzed sustained tech-industry-and-academia tensions over corporate research independence.

## Background

Born in Eritrea, raised in the US after family fled to Ireland during the Eritrean-Ethiopian war. PhD in computer vision from Stanford (2017, advisor Fei-Fei Li). Postdoc at Microsoft Research. Joined Google in 2018, co-leading the Ethical AI team with Margaret Mitchell.

Fired by Google in December 2020 over the Stochastic Parrots paper and related disagreements about research review processes. Founded DAIR (Distributed AI Research Institute) in December 2021 as an independent research organization focused on AI's effects on marginalized communities.

## Contributions

### Gender Shades (2018)
Co-authored with Joy Buolamwini. Demonstrated severe accuracy disparities in commercial facial-analysis systems (IBM, Microsoft, Face++): error rates up to 34% higher for darker-skinned women compared to lighter-skinned men. The paper drove industry changes and significantly contributed to bans on government facial-recognition use in several jurisdictions.

### Stochastic Parrots (2021)
Co-author of the paper critiquing large language models. The internal Google review processes around this paper became the proximate cause of her firing.

### DAIR (2021–)
Founded as a model for independent, community-focused AI research. Specifically positioned as an alternative to corporate research labs and to academic institutions with heavy industry ties. Focus areas: ethics of AI in African, Black diaspora, and other underrepresented contexts; data work transparency; algorithmic accountability.

## Positions

`perspective: cautious` (with strong focus on near-term harms and structural critiques)

Gebru's positions emphasise:
- Documented current harms from AI deployment (bias, surveillance, labor exploitation, environmental costs) deserve priority over speculative future risks.
- The "AI x-risk" / longtermist framing serves industry interests by directing attention away from present harms.
- AI development is not neutral — its current trajectory reflects who funds, who builds, and whom it serves.
- Many "AI" framings obscure exploitative labor (data annotation, content moderation), often in the Global South.

She has been among the most public critics of effective-altruism / longtermist framings of AI risk.

## Reception

Polarized:

- *Within FAccT, AI ethics, and broader critical-tech communities*: foundational figure; her firing is treated as a watershed moment.
- *Within mainstream ML*: respected technically; the controversy itself has been processed differently by different parties.
- *From x-risk and alignment communities*: substantial disagreement on framing and priorities; some criticism of her dismissal of x-risk concerns.
- *Public discourse*: significant voice; cited extensively in critical-AI journalism and policy debates.
