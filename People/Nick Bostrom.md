# Nick Bostrom

> Philosopher who articulated existential risk as a research priority and authored *Superintelligence* (2014), the book most responsible for moving AI x-risk into mainstream discourse. Founded and led the Future of Humanity Institute at Oxford from 2005 until its dissolution in 2024.

## Background

Born 1973 in Sweden. Eclectic education spanning physics, computational neuroscience, philosophy, and AI. PhD in philosophy from LSE (2000). Founded the Future of Humanity Institute (FHI) at Oxford in 2005. FHI was wound down by Oxford in April 2024 following internal university tensions.

## Contributions

### Existential risk framework
Bostrom's 2002 paper "Existential Risks: Analyzing Human Extinction Scenarios" formalized the category and influenced subsequent x-risk scholarship. He defined existential risk as outcomes annihilating Earth-originating intelligent life or permanently and drastically curtailing its potential.

defined-by:: [[Existential Risk]]
coined-by:: [[Existential Catastrophe]]

### Superintelligence (2014)
The book systematized arguments about superintelligent AI as a potential x-risk: paths to superintelligence, take-off dynamics, control problem (alignment), value loading, and treacherous turn scenarios. Provided much of the conceptual vocabulary subsequent AI safety work uses.

### Simulation Argument
Bostrom's 2003 paper argues that at least one of three propositions is true: (1) civilizations almost always go extinct before achieving simulation capability; (2) civilizations capable of running ancestor simulations almost always choose not to; or (3) we are almost certainly living in a simulation. The argument has been influential in philosophy of mind and computer science contexts.

### Deep Utopia (2024)
Recent book reframing his focus from preventing dystopia to articulating what a positive long-run future requires — engaging the question of meaning and purpose in a world where AI handles instrumentally important tasks.

## Positions

`perspective: cautious`

Bostrom is widely considered a paradigm cautious / x-risk-prioritizing thinker, though his actual positions are more nuanced than the popular framing. He has explicitly resisted both naive optimism and naive doomerism, and has paid more attention than most x-risk thinkers to positive long-run futures.

proponent-of:: [[Differential Technological Development]]

## Reception

Highly influential and highly controversial. Reception:

- *Within x-risk / longtermist communities*: foundational figure.
- *From mainstream AI*: mixed historically; capability progress in 2020s has moved some former skeptics toward partial agreement with Bostrom's framings.
- *From FAccT and AI ethics critics*: critiqued for longtermist framing crowding out near-term concerns and (some argue) for racism in early personal correspondence revealed in 2023 — a controversy that contributed to FHI's dissolution.
- *From philosophy*: respected within philosophy of probability, ethics, and existential risk; less central to mainstream metaphysics or epistemology.

Bostrom's 1996 email containing a racist passage, apologized for, surfaced publicly in early 2023 and was significant in subsequent FHI-Oxford tensions.
