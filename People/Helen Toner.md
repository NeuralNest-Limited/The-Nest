# Helen Toner

> AI policy researcher at Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET); former OpenAI board member; significant figure in the November 2023 OpenAI governance crisis. A bridge figure between technical AI safety community and US government policy circles.

## Background

Australian. Background combining philanthropy (formerly at Open Philanthropy) and policy research. Director of Strategy and Foundational Research Grants at CSET (Georgetown University), a non-partisan policy research center focused on emerging technology and national security.

Joined the OpenAI non-profit board in 2021; remained until being removed in November 2023 alongside other board members after the brief firing and reinstatement of CEO Sam Altman.

## Contributions

### AI policy research at CSET
Lead on a body of CSET work covering:
- US-China AI policy and capability comparison.
- AI workforce and immigration policy.
- Export controls and compute governance.
- Safety frameworks and evaluation methodologies.

CSET's policy briefings are widely-read in US executive branch and Congressional staff circles, influencing positions on the 2023 Biden Executive Order on AI and on export-control regimes (the 2022 and 2023 chip export controls).

### OpenAI board service and the November 2023 events
Joined the board representing a non-commercial perspective consistent with OpenAI's non-profit governance structure. The board's November 2023 firing of Sam Altman was reversed within days under employee and investor pressure. Toner subsequently spoke publicly (notably in a 2024 TED interview) about the board's reasoning, citing concerns about candor and pace of safety-relevant decision-making.

The episode became a touchstone for debates about whether nonprofit governance structures can constrain commercial frontier AI development.

## Positions

`perspective: safety-pragmatist`

Toner's positions, as articulable from public writing:

- Frontier AI development is genuinely high-stakes; governance is needed but careful design matters.
- Safety considerations must include geopolitical dimensions; ignoring competitive dynamics in policy design produces brittle outcomes.
- Compute governance and export controls are tractable policy levers.
- Industry self-governance is insufficient but not zero-value.

## Reception

- *Within DC policy*: respected; CSET's work has been highly influential.
- *Within AI safety community*: respected for combining technical understanding with policy sophistication.
- *From accelerationist quarters*: critiqued, especially after the November 2023 events.
- *Public profile*: significantly raised after the OpenAI episode and her subsequent public discussion of it.
