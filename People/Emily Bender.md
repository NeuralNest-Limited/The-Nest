# Emily M. Bender

> Professor of Linguistics at University of Washington; co-author of the influential 2021 "Stochastic Parrots" paper; a leading critical voice on LLM capabilities discourse, AI hype, and the political economy of AI development.

## Background

PhD in Linguistics from Stanford (2000). Faculty at University of Washington since 2003, currently Howard and Frances Nostrand Endowed Professor in the Department of Linguistics. Directs the Computational Linguistics Laboratory.

affiliated-with:: [[University of Washington]]

## Contributions

### Computational linguistics foundations
Long-running work on grammar engineering, multilingual NLP, and the Bender Rule (an ethical practice of always naming the languages in NLP papers, addressing default-English assumptions). Authored *Linguistic Fundamentals for NLP* textbooks.

### "Stochastic Parrots" (2021)
First author of the FAccT paper that became the proximate cause of Google's firing of Timnit Gebru and Margaret Mitchell. Articulated systemic critiques of large language models: environmental costs, training-data biases, fluent-text-without-understanding misperceptions, and research-direction opportunity costs.

authored-by:: [[bender-stochastic-parrots-2021]]
coined-by:: [[Stochastic Parrots]]

### "Octopus argument" and related framings
Bender's "Climbing towards NLU" (2020, with Alexander Koller) and related works articulated the case that LLMs trained on text alone do not achieve understanding. The "octopus thought experiment" became a touchstone in debates about LLM capabilities.

coined-by:: [[Octopus Argument]]

### Mystery AI Hype Theater 3000 (2022+)
Podcast (with Alex Hanna) that critiques AI hype in mainstream tech reporting. Regularly listened to in critical-tech communities.

### *The AI Con* (2025)
Book-length critique of AI industry hype patterns, co-authored with Alex Hanna of DAIR.

authored-by:: [[The AI Con 2025]]

## Positions

`perspective: cautious` (with strong near-term-harms focus)

Bender's positions:
- Documented current harms from AI deployment deserve priority over speculative future risks.
- The "AI" framing often obscures specific socio-technical systems with specific human authors, labor, and accountability gaps.
- LLM "understanding" claims are linguistically and philosophically unsupported.
- Industry self-regulation has structurally failed; substantive regulation is needed.
- The x-risk / longtermist framing serves to direct attention away from present harms.

She is among the most consistent and prominent critics of the AI x-risk framing, often pairing critique with respectful disagreement.
