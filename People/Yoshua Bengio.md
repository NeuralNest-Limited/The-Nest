# Yoshua Bengio

> Founding figure of deep learning; recipient of the 2018 ACM Turing Award (with Hinton and LeCun); chair of the international State of AI Safety report; since 2023 among the most vocal academic voices urging caution on frontier AI development.

## Background

Born 1964 in Paris; raised in Canada. PhD from McGill (1991). Faculty at Université de Montréal since 1993. Founded Mila (Quebec Artificial Intelligence Institute) in 1993; under his direction it became one of the largest deep learning research clusters globally.

affiliated-with:: [[Mila]]

## Contributions

### Deep learning foundations
Bengio's group contributed centrally to: neural language models (a 2003 paper on neural probabilistic language models is foundational); attention mechanisms (Bahdanau-Cho-Bengio 2014, precursor to Transformer attention); generative adversarial networks (Goodfellow's PhD work in Bengio's lab); and many representation-learning techniques.

### Turing Award (2018)
Awarded with Hinton and LeCun for "conceptual and engineering breakthroughs that have made deep neural networks a critical component of computing."

### Shift to safety (2023–present)
In 2023, Bengio publicly stated that he had updated significantly on AI risk after observing capability progress in GPT-4 and successors. Subsequent activities:
- Signed FLI pause letter (March 2023) and CAIS extinction-risk statement (May 2023).
- Co-authored "Managing AI Risks in an Era of Rapid Progress" (Science, 2024) calling for major safety investment and government oversight.
- Chairs the *International Scientific Report on the Safety of Advanced AI* (UK-commissioned, multi-government endorsed, 2024+).
- Founded LawZero (2024), a non-profit pursuing safety-oriented AI research.

## Positions

`perspective: cautious`

Bengio explicitly identifies as having updated from a position closer to optimist toward cautious. He distinguishes near-term harms (which he treats as serious) from longer-term loss-of-control risks (which he treats as plausible enough to warrant priority).

## Reception

The 2023 shift was treated as significant inside the field — a "godfather of deep learning" updating toward x-risk concern was widely covered. Reception:
- *Within safety community*: welcomed as legitimating long-standing concerns.
- *From accelerationists / LeCun's camp*: critiqued as alarmism.
- *Within academic ML*: mixed — many appreciated the seriousness; some questioned whether capability evidence supports the updated views.
