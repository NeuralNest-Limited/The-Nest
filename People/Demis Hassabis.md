# Demis Hassabis

> Co-founder (2010) and CEO of DeepMind (now Google DeepMind); chess prodigy turned game designer turned neuroscientist turned AI executive. Recipient of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (with John Jumper and David Baker) for protein-structure prediction with AlphaFold.

## Background

Born 1976 in London. Co-founder of game studio Elixir Studios in his 20s. Returned to academia for PhD in cognitive neuroscience at UCL (2009). Founded DeepMind in 2010 with Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman. Acquired by Google in 2014; merged with Google Brain to form Google DeepMind in 2023, with Hassabis as CEO of the combined entity.

affiliated-with:: [[Google DeepMind]]

## Contributions

### DeepMind milestones (under his leadership)
- DQN (2013–2015): deep Q-networks playing Atari from pixels.
- AlphaGo (2016): defeat of world champion Lee Sedol.
- AlphaZero (2017): general game-playing system.
- AlphaFold (2018–2021): protein-structure prediction at near-experimental accuracy.
- Gemini (2023–): frontier multimodal models.
- AlphaMissense, GraphCast, AlphaProof, and many domain-specialized scientific AI systems.

### Nobel Prize 2024
Awarded jointly with John Jumper (DeepMind) and David Baker (Washington) for protein-structure prediction. The award is the first Nobel substantially recognizing AI methods (concurrent with the 2024 Physics Nobel for Hinton and Hopfield).

## Positions

`perspective: cautious` (with optimistic application emphasis)

Hassabis has consistently framed DeepMind's mission as scientific discovery via AI. He signed the CAIS extinction-risk statement (2023). His public statements emphasise:

- AI safety as a serious priority requiring scientific investment.
- Government and international coordination as necessary supplements to industry effort.
- Scientific applications (biology, materials, climate) as a primary positive case for AI.
- Skepticism of immediate-doom framings paired with seriousness about long-run alignment.

## Reception

- *Within ML*: high standing; Nobel recognition removed any remaining question about scientific seriousness.
- *Within safety community*: respected; viewed as one of the safer frontier-lab leaders.
- *From civil society*: mixed; concerns about Google's market position and the merger's effects on safety culture (Google Brain and DeepMind had different cultures pre-merger).
- *From accelerationists*: critiqued for slowness; DeepMind has historically released fewer products than OpenAI or Anthropic.
