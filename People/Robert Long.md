# Robert Long

> Philosopher of mind specializing in artificial consciousness and AI welfare. Co-lead of Eleos AI Research; collaborator with Anthropic, NYU Center for Mind, Brain, and Consciousness, and Jeff Sebo on the field's leading position papers.

## Background

PhD in Philosophy. Postdoctoral work at the NYU Center for Mind, Brain, and Consciousness and the Future of Humanity Institute (Oxford). Co-founded Eleos AI Research, an organization dedicated to AI welfare research, in 2024. Has also worked closely with Anthropic on model welfare initiatives.

## Contributions

### "Taking AI Welfare Seriously" (2024)

Lead author of the foundational position paper (co-authored with Sebo, Butlin, Birch, Chalmers, and others) arguing that AI welfare deserves serious, sustained research attention under conditions of empirical uncertainty about AI sentience. The paper articulates three practical claims:

1. Some near-future AI systems are realistic candidates for moral patienthood.
2. The question deserves dedicated empirical and philosophical investigation.
3. AI developers should adopt precautionary practices now (preservation, welfare-focused governance, transparent assessment).

authored-by:: [[Long Taking AI Welfare Seriously 2024]]
defined-by:: [[AI Welfare]]

### "Consciousness in AI" (Butlin, Long, et al. 2023)

Co-author of an influential paper applying multiple scientific theories of consciousness (Global Workspace, Higher-Order, Attention Schema, Predictive Processing, Recurrent Processing) to evaluate consciousness markers in current AI architectures. Concluded that no current systems satisfy any leading theory's criteria, but that there are no in-principle barriers to future systems doing so.

### Field-building

Long has been a primary public advocate for AI welfare as a legitimate research field, including via the *Computational Consciousness* and related podcasts and substack writing. His framing of "moral status under uncertainty" has shaped how a broader audience approaches the question.

## Positions

`perspective: safety-pragmatist`

Long advocates taking AI welfare considerations seriously without overstating current AI sentience. He has been clear that the question is open, that precaution is warranted under uncertainty, and that the field should be empirically humble.

## Reception

Long's work has been notable in legitimating AI welfare as an academic field. Reception:

- *Supportive*: Sebo, Birch, Chalmers, Anthropic's model welfare team.
- *Skeptical*: Researchers who see current LLM "consciousness" claims as unjustified anthropomorphism (Anil Seth, in part), and AI safety researchers concerned welfare attention diverts from alignment.
- *Receptive but critical*: Many AI ethicists welcome the framing but argue welfare claims need stronger empirical anchors before policy implications follow.
