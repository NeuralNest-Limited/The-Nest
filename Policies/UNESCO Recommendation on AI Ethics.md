# UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

> The first global standard-setting instrument on AI ethics, adopted unanimously by UNESCO's 193 member states in November 2021. Provides values-based framework intended to inform national AI policies. Non-binding ("soft law") but with implementation monitoring through Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM) and Ethical Impact Assessments (EIA).

## Jurisdiction and authority

Global. Adopted by UNESCO's General Conference (193 member states). UNESCO has no direct enforcement authority; the Recommendation operates through:
- National-level adoption and implementation.
- Capacity-building support (UNESCO RAM tool, training).
- Reporting and monitoring through UNESCO mechanisms.

## Status and timeline

- **November 23, 2021**: Adopted by UNESCO General Conference.
- **2022-2024**: Implementation support through Readiness Assessment Methodology; pilot national readiness assessments.
- **Ongoing**: UNESCO Global AI Ethics Forum and other implementation activities.

## Substantive provisions

The Recommendation comprises:

### Values (four)
- Respect, protection, and promotion of human rights, fundamental freedoms, and human dignity.
- Living in peaceful, just, and interconnected societies.
- Ensuring diversity and inclusiveness.
- Environment and ecosystem flourishing.

### Principles (ten)
- Proportionality and do-no-harm.
- Safety and security.
- Fairness and non-discrimination.
- Sustainability.
- Right to privacy and data protection.
- Human oversight and determination.
- Transparency and explainability.
- Responsibility and accountability.
- Awareness and literacy.
- Multi-stakeholder and adaptive governance.

### Policy action areas (eleven)
Detailed guidance for member states on: ethical impact assessment, governance frameworks, data policy, development and international cooperation, environment, gender, culture, education, communication and information, economy and labor, health and social well-being.

### Implementation tools

- **Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM)**: helps countries assess their state of AI readiness across legal, social, technical, and ethical dimensions.
- **Ethical Impact Assessment (EIA)**: methodology for assessing AI system impact.
- Reporting mechanisms within UNESCO structure.

## Reception

- *Among governments*: substantial uptake especially in middle-income and developing country contexts where domestic AI policy infrastructure is less developed; UNESCO's framework provides a credible reference.
- *Among developed jurisdictions*: less central; supplementary to OECD AI Principles, EU AI Act, and national frameworks.
- *Civil society*: largely positive; values-based framing is open to interpretation that allows diverse implementations.
- *Critiques*: 
  - Non-binding nature limits effectiveness.
  - Some concepts (e.g., "ensure beneficial outcomes") are vague enough to permit very different implementations.
  - Tension with member states whose practices conflict with stated values.
