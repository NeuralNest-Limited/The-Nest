# UK AI Safety Institute Mandate

> The framework establishing the world's first national AI safety institute, announced at the November 2023 Bletchley AI Safety Summit. Mandates the institute's evaluation and coordination functions; relies on voluntary cooperation with frontier AI labs rather than statutory authority.

## Jurisdiction and authority

UK government. AISI sits within the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT). Operates with significant operational independence but without independent statutory standing.

## Status and timeline

- **November 1-2, 2023**: UK hosted the Bletchley AI Safety Summit; announcement of AISI.
- **November 2023**: AISI began operations.
- **2024-2025**: International AISI Network formed (UK, US, Singapore, Japan, Canada, France, Korea, Australia, EU, India).
- **Ongoing**: subject to UK government budget cycles; the 2024 Labour government affirmed continuation.

policy_status: enacted

## Substantive provisions

The "mandate" is a composite of UK government statements rather than a single act. Key elements:

### Mission and remit
- Evaluate frontier AI systems for safety.
- Develop the science of AI safety evaluation.
- Coordinate internationally on safety methodology and findings.
- Advise UK government on AI risks and policy.

### Lab cooperation
Voluntary memoranda of understanding (not statutory) with major frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind initially) for pre-deployment access. The voluntary nature has been a point of policy debate.

### Public reporting
AISI publishes evaluation methodology and (sometimes) findings, with redactions for capability-sensitive details.

### International coordination
The Bletchley, Seoul, and Paris AI Safety Summit series; ongoing International Scientific Report on the Safety of Advanced AI; AISI Network coordination.

governs:: [[UK Frontier AI Evaluation]]

### Enforcement and limits

- No statutory authority to compel lab cooperation.
- No power to block deployment.
- Recommendations to UK government and labs; persuasion-based influence.
- Future legislation (an AI bill anticipated 2025-2026 under the Labour government) could formalize authorities.

## Reception

- *International*: Model emulated by many jurisdictions (US AISI within NIST, Singapore, Japan, Canada, EU AI Office partial overlap, Korea, etc.).
- *Within UK*: bipartisan support during 2023-2024; continued under Labour.
- *Industry*: cooperative posture maintained but voluntary nature is structural.
- *Critique*: civil society and academic critics argue voluntary access without statutory backing limits effectiveness; counter-argument is that voluntary cooperation enables more substantive access than adversarial regulation would.
- *Geographic critique*: AISI's reach is global by virtue of frontier-lab cooperation; but accountability remains UK-domestic, raising questions about whose interests AISI ultimately represents.
