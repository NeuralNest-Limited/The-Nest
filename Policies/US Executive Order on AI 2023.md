# US Executive Order on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy AI (EO 14110)

> Biden administration October 2023 executive order — the most ambitious unified US federal action on AI to that date, mandating reporting requirements, evaluation standards, and federal-agency activities. Revoked by Trump administration in January 2025; some implementing structures (notably US AISI within NIST) persisted under different framing.

## Jurisdiction and authority

US federal government. Issued by the President under executive authority; binds federal executive agencies; reaches the private sector primarily through reporting requirements tied to the Defense Production Act.

## Status and timeline

- **October 30, 2023**: Signed by President Biden as EO 14110.
- **January 20, 2025**: Revoked by incoming Trump administration as part of broader EO rescissions.
- **January 23, 2025**: Trump executive order "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in AI" framed a different federal AI policy posture.
- Subsequent: ongoing reframing through 2025-2026; some EO 14110 structures (US AISI within NIST) survived under modified mandates.

policy_status: superseded

## Substantive provisions

EO 14110 was unusual in length and scope (~110 pages of operative text). Major elements:

### Reporting under Defense Production Act
Companies training dual-use foundation models above specified compute thresholds (10^26 FLOPs initial threshold) required to report:
- Training plans.
- Cybersecurity protections for model weights.
- Results of red-team evaluations of dangerous capabilities (CBRN, cyber).

### NIST AI standards
- Development of AI Risk Management Framework standards.
- Foundation for what became US AISI within NIST.

### Civil rights and consumer protection
Direction to federal agencies on AI-driven discrimination, employment AI use, housing AI, etc.

### Workforce and immigration
Streamlined visa processes for AI talent.

### International engagement
Direction to State Department on international AI coordination.

### Enforcement
Federal agency action. The reporting requirements under DPA carried the strongest enforcement mechanism but applied to a small number of companies. Most provisions operated through agency rulemaking and federal procurement.

## Reception

- *Supportive view (safety / civil society)*: A meaningful federal action establishing reporting requirements where none existed; created US AISI within NIST.
- *Industry mixed*: large companies generally compliant with reporting; civil society advocates wanted stronger provisions.
- *Conservative / accelerationist critique*: too prescriptive; uses Defense Production Act in a stretched manner; creates compliance burden.
- *Progressive critique*: insufficient teeth on civil-rights provisions; over-emphasized x-risk concerns relative to current harms.
