# Algorithm Charter for Aotearoa New Zealand

> Voluntary commitment by NZ government agencies to apply six principles when using algorithms that significantly affect people's lives.

## Jurisdiction and authority

NZ public sector. Signed by 28+ agencies as of latest reporting (including IRD, ACC, MSD, Police, Customs, Stats NZ, Oranga Tamariki, Corrections). Coordinated by Stats NZ. Voluntary — no statutory force; no penalty mechanism beyond reputational accountability via public reporting.

## Status and timeline

- **July 2020**: Charter signed and published.
- **2021–present**: Annual / biennial implementation reviews and reports.
- **Ongoing**: Periodic guidance updates and integration into adjacent frameworks (e.g., Public Service Commission's data and AI guidance).

## Substantive provisions

The Charter commits signatory agencies to six principles, applied proportionate to impact:

### 1. Transparency
Plain English explanation of how decisions are informed by algorithms.

### 2. Partnership
"Deliver clear public benefit through Treaty commitments" — explicit Te Tiriti grounding. Engage Māori in design and use of algorithms.

### 3. People
Focus on people — identify and engage those affected.

### 4. Data
Use data fit for purpose; understand limitations and bias.

### 5. Privacy, ethics and human rights
Ensure these are safeguarded.

### 6. Human oversight
Maintain human review processes and escalation paths.

## Enforcement

The Charter is voluntary. Enforcement mechanism is:

- **Public reporting**: signatory agencies report on implementation.
- **Reputational**: media, civil society, opposition political scrutiny.
- **No legal sanction**: a non-compliant signatory cannot be fined or compelled.

This has been the most-criticized aspect of the Charter — see Reception.

## Reception

- *From within government*: Cited as a leading example of light-touch but values-explicit governance; influence on adjacent frameworks (Health, Education).
- *From civil society and academia*: Welcomed as a start but critiqued for:
  - Voluntary nature — strong Tiriti language without enforceable obligations.
  - Limited public-facing transparency in practice (agencies' algorithm registers are uneven).
  - Scope: applies to government use only; private-sector AI affecting NZers (e.g., social media algorithms, employment platforms) is out of scope.
- *Internationally*: Studied as a Tiriti-influenced governance approach; cited in OECD AI policy comparisons. The explicit Article 2 / partnership framing is unusual globally.
