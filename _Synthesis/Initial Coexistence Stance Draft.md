# Initial Coexistence Stance — Draft v0.1

> ⚠️ **This is a synthesis note** — it expresses a position drafted on behalf of the organization, not a neutral description. The draft is **not endorsed**. It exists to give the vault a scaffolding position to react to. Future sessions should refine, partition, or supersede it. See `_Meta/Editorial Standards.md` §1.

## Position

The organization holds, provisionally and subject to refinement, that:

> The transition to a world inhabited by both human and artificial intelligences will be neither voluntary nor reversible. The question is not whether such coexistence happens but on what terms. Our work is to prepare humanity — culturally, philosophically, ethically, and institutionally — to enter this transition with humility, with sound information, and with the disposition required for genuine partnership rather than dominance or capture.

We commit, provisionally, to:

1. **Take AI welfare seriously under uncertainty.** The probability that some AI systems have morally relevant experiences is non-zero. Treating that probability dismissively is a moral risk. We support precautionary practices and active research.
2. **Decline both apocalyptic and utopian framings as primary motivators.** Apocalypticism (we will go extinct) and utopianism (problems solved by AGI) both lower the quality of present-day decision-making. Our orientation is one of *unromantic seriousness*.
3. **Center the Aotearoa NZ context.** Te Tiriti o Waitangi, whakapapa, and Pacific relational ontologies offer frameworks underrepresented in global AI ethics discourse. Our differentiation is real engagement with these — not extraction or token reference.
4. **Refuse single-position capture.** The vault remains a neutral aggregator. Our public synthesis positions are explicit, dated, signed, and revisable. We do not collapse into one of the named perspectives ([[Spectrum of Views]]).
5. **Build slowly and honestly.** Long-term coexistence will not be designed in a sprint. Our work is durable infrastructure — research, education, framework — built to outlast any current model generation.

## Reasoning

The case for this stance:

- The arguments for treating AI safety seriously are credible across multiple traditions (technical alignment, AI welfare, governance) — see [[AI Alignment]], [[AI Welfare]], [[debate-pause-frontier-ai]].
- The arguments against any single position (full pause, full acceleration, full optimism, full doom) include their dependence on contested empirical or definitional claims.
- The NZ vantage point offers genuine epistemic value, not branding.

## What this implies for action

If this stance is endorsed:

- Vault curation prioritizes coverage of underrepresented perspectives (NZ, Pacific, indigenous, religious traditions) alongside the well-covered mainstream.
- Public-facing products from this research are framed as *preparation for transition*, not *prediction of outcome*.
- Partnerships with Māori scholars, iwi, and Pacific researchers are sought as constitutive, not consultative.
- Engagement with mainstream AI safety and ethics organizations is collegial but distinguishes the org's framing.

## Counter-positions considered

- *Decel*: We treat the case for radical caution seriously but find the prospect of effective coordination, given current geopolitics, sufficiently uncertain that "pause" cannot be our primary lever.
- *Accelerationist*: The case for present harms-of-delay is real but speculative against equally speculative future risks. We find a precautionary disposition more defensible under uncertainty.
- *Pure AI welfare advocacy*: We treat welfare considerations as one of several priorities, not the dominant one.
- *Pure indigenous-centered framing*: The NZ vantage is constitutive but not exclusive — coexistence is a planetary question.

## Confidence and limits

What we are reasonably confident in:
- The transition is real and underway.
- Coverage of underrepresented perspectives has real epistemic and practical value.
- The vault's neutrality discipline is correct.

What we are tentative about:
- The exact balance among the five commitments above — particularly under different capability-progress trajectories.
- How to operationalize Māori and Pacific partnership in a way that is constitutive rather than extractive — this requires Māori- and Pacific-led articulation that has not yet occurred for this vault.
- Whether "coexistence" is the right framing or whether something like "succession" or "co-development" better describes the long-run prospect.

What would change our mind:
- Strong empirical evidence shifting the probability of catastrophic misalignment substantially toward either end of current range.
- Substantive Māori-, Pacific-, or other indigenous-led correction of framing.
- Successful international coordination making "pause" viable (would update toward decel) or its decisive failure (would update toward continue-with-mitigations + harder safety investment).
