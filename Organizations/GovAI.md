# Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI)

> Independent research center focused on governance of advanced AI, drawing on policy, international relations, and strategic studies. Among the most academically rigorous voices in AI governance, with broad policymaker reach.

## Founding and structure

Founded 2017 by Allan Dafoe within Oxford's Future of Humanity Institute. Operated under FHI through 2020. Spun out as independent organization in 2021. Now operates as UK-registered non-profit headquartered in Oxford, with researchers and fellows distributed across institutions globally.

affiliated-with:: [[Allan Dafoe]] (founder; subsequently moved to DeepMind)

## Mission and focus

Research focus: how international, national, and corporate governance should respond to transformative AI. Distinctive emphasis on:

- Strategic and competitive dynamics of AI development.
- International coordination mechanisms.
- Standards and evaluations as policy tools.
- Compute and chip governance.
- Long-term governance scenarios.

Less focused on near-term ethics applications (bias, fairness, etc.); more focused on transformative-AI governance.

## Notable outputs

- Dafoe et al. *AI Governance: A Research Agenda* (2018, foundational document).
- Papers on AI strategy and IR.
- Workshops convening policymakers, academics, and industry.
- Significant contributions to UK AISI's founding rationale and operating framework.
- Researchers' fellowship programs (GovAI Fellowship, Summer Fellowship) — placed many subsequent AI governance professionals.

## Funding and influence

Philanthropic funding (Open Philanthropy major), with some research grants. Influence operates through:

- Academic publication quality.
- Researcher placement into government and industry policy roles.
- Convening role between policy and research communities.

## Reception and critique

- *Within governance / policy*: highly respected for technical rigor.
- *Within safety community*: central node for governance-side work.
- *From FAccT-adjacent critics*: critiqued (alongside the broader "EA / longtermist" governance cluster) for over-emphasis on transformative-AI scenarios.
- *Methodological critique*: some critics argue strategic / IR framings of AI governance import assumptions from competitive geopolitics that may not be productive.
