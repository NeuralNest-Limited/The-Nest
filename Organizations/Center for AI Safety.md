# Center for AI Safety (CAIS)

> Non-profit founded by Dan Hendrycks to reduce catastrophic and existential risks from AI through research, communication, and field-building. Best known for organizing the May 2023 single-sentence "Statement on AI Risk" signed by hundreds of AI scientists and executives.

## Founding and structure

Founded 2022 by Dan Hendrycks (formerly UC Berkeley PhD), with seed support from Open Philanthropy and other funders. Based in San Francisco.

## Mission and focus

Stated mission: reduce catastrophic and societal-scale risks from AI. Methods:

1. **Research**: capability benchmarks, risk taxonomies, alignment-relevant techniques.
2. **Communication**: public communications about AI risk.
3. **Field-building**: support pipeline of researchers into AI safety.
4. **Compute and infrastructure**: providing safety researchers with access to compute and shared infrastructure.

## Notable outputs

- **"Statement on AI Risk" (May 2023)**: single-sentence statement: "Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war." Signed by Hinton, Bengio, Hassabis, Altman, Amodei, several Turing Award winners, and hundreds of researchers. Significantly elevated the perceived legitimacy of x-risk framing.
- **"An Overview of Catastrophic AI Risks" (Hendrycks, Mazeika, Woodside 2023)**: comprehensive risk taxonomy widely cited.
- **MMLU and related benchmarks**: Hendrycks-led benchmarks predate CAIS but inform its evaluation work.
- **WMDP benchmark** (Weapons of Mass Destruction Proxy, 2024): dangerous-knowledge capability evaluation.
- **SafeBench**: safety-focused evaluation infrastructure.
- **CAIS Compute Cluster**: shared compute for safety researchers.

## Funding and influence

Philanthropic funding (Open Philanthropy, others). Influence operates primarily through:

- The 2023 Statement on AI Risk as a Schelling point for mainstream acknowledgment.
- Hendrycks' personal academic and policy influence.
- Benchmarks adopted as standard evaluations across the field.

## Reception and critique

- *Within AI safety community*: respected; the 2023 statement is treated as a turning point.
- *Within mainstream ML*: substantial signatory base from the field itself.
- *From FAccT and ethics critics*: critiqued for the x-risk framing crowding out near-term harms; the brevity of the 2023 statement allowed varied interpretations.
- *From accelerationist quarters*: critiqued as alarmist.
