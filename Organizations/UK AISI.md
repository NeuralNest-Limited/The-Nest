# UK AI Safety Institute (AISI)

> First national AI safety institute globally; created November 2023 at the Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit announcement. Initially housed within the UK government's Department for Science, Innovation and Technology. Conducts evaluations of frontier AI systems and convenes international AI safety institute network.

## Founding and structure

Announced at the UK-hosted Bletchley AI Safety Summit (November 1-2, 2023), the first major international gathering specifically on AI safety. Operates as part of UK government with significant operational independence.

Established with initial budget commitments around £100M and a brief to:
1. Develop the science of AI safety evaluation.
2. Evaluate frontier models pre-deployment (with cooperating labs).
3. Coordinate internationally with peer institutes.
4. Inform UK and international AI policy.

## Mission and focus

Operating principles emphasise:
- **Pre-deployment evaluation**: agreements with major frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind initially) for pre-release access to evaluate models.
- **Dangerous capability evaluation**: focus on biological, chemical, cyber, and autonomous-capability risks.
- **Public reporting**: publish evaluation methodologies and findings (with redactions for sensitive details).
- **International coordination**: convenes the International AISI Network including peer institutes.

## Notable outputs

- **Bletchley Declaration (November 2023)**: signed by 28 countries (including US, EU members, China, India) acknowledging frontier AI risks.
- **AI Safety Summits**: Bletchley (UK 2023), Seoul (South Korea 2024), Paris (France 2025).
- **International Scientific Report on the Safety of Advanced AI** (chaired by Yoshua Bengio): expert-consensus report on AI risks, modeled on IPCC.
- Pre-deployment evaluations of GPT-4o, Claude 3, Gemini, others.
- Methodological publications on evaluation design.

## Key people

- **Chair**: rotated through Ian Hogarth (founding chair, 2023–2024) and subsequent leadership.
- **Director**: rotated.
- Research staff drawn from AI safety community, including former GovAI, Apollo, METR affiliates.

## Funding and influence

UK government funded. Influence:

- First-mover status — significant influence on subsequent AISI designs (US, EU members, Japan, others).
- Coordinating role for international AI safety policy.
- Trusted pre-deployment evaluator role with major frontier labs.
- The International Scientific Report aims to function as IPCC-equivalent for AI risk consensus.

## Reception and critique

- *Internationally*: model widely emulated.
- *UK government cycles*: subject to budget and political pressure as governments change.
- *From frontier labs*: cooperative relationship has held but is voluntary and conditional.
- *From safety community*: positive overall; concerns about regulatory capture and the limits of voluntary pre-deployment access.
- *From civil society*: mixed; some argue more democratic accountability is needed.
