# Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI)

> Academic AI safety research center at UC Berkeley, founded 2016 by Stuart Russell. Pursues a research agenda centered on AI systems that are uncertain about human values and learn them from observation — the "provably beneficial AI" framework.

## Founding and structure

Founded 2016 with seed support from Open Philanthropy. Hosted at UC Berkeley (with collaboration ties to Cornell and University of Michigan). Operates as an academic research center; produces papers, hosts students, and engages in policy advisory work.

affiliated-with:: [[UC Berkeley]]

## Mission and focus

Research agenda: develop "provably beneficial" AI systems — systems whose objective is to maximize realization of human preferences but which are uncertain about those preferences and learn them from human behavior.

Core technical research lines:

1. **Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning (CIRL)**: formal framework for value-uncertain AI cooperation with humans.
2. **Assistance games**: extensions of CIRL.
3. **Corrigibility and shutdown problems**.
4. **Reward modeling under uncertainty**.
5. **Multi-agent and human-AI interaction**.

## Notable outputs

- *Human Compatible* (Russell 2019) — book-length articulation.
- CIRL paper (Hadfield-Menell et al. 2016).
- Many subsequent papers on assistance games, off-switch problems, and value learning.
- CHAI alumni have populated AI safety teams at Anthropic, DeepMind, OpenAI, and elsewhere.

## Funding and influence

Funded by Open Philanthropy and Berkeley resources. Outsized influence relative to size, through:
- Russell's public profile and policy engagement.
- High-quality publication output.
- Pipeline placing safety-trained researchers into industry labs.

## Reception and critique

- *Within ML*: respected academic work; CIRL framework cited extensively.
- *Within safety community*: respected; sometimes critiqued for the gap between theoretical CIRL work and practical alignment of deployed LLMs.
- *From CAIS reframing critics (Drexler)*: critiqued for assuming unified-agent framing that may not match deployed AI.
- *Tractability concerns*: open question whether CIRL-style approaches scale to frontier models.
