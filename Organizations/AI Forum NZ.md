# AI Forum of New Zealand

> Industry-led non-profit organization founded in 2017 to coordinate AI activity, policy engagement, and economic-impact analysis in Aotearoa New Zealand.

## Founding and structure

Founded 2017 as a project of NZTech (an industry membership body for the technology sector). Operates as a member-supported non-profit. Membership includes major NZ firms, multinational tech companies operating in NZ, universities, and government agencies. Funded primarily through membership fees and sponsored research projects.

Headquartered in Wellington with members across NZ.

## Mission and focus

Stated focus areas:

1. Coordinating NZ's AI ecosystem (industry, academia, government).
2. Publishing analyses of AI economic impact in NZ.
3. Policy engagement — submissions on AI-relevant legislation and regulatory consultations.
4. Public education on AI topics.

## Notable outputs

- **"Towards Our Intelligent Future: An AI Roadmap for New Zealand" (2018)**: First major national report assessing AI's potential economic impact on NZ and policy implications. Estimated significant GDP contributions from AI adoption; influential in framing subsequent policy conversations.
- **Annual State of AI reports**: Tracking industry adoption, skills, and policy environment.
- **Policy submissions**: To the Productivity Commission, Privacy Commissioner consultations, and parliamentary inquiries on AI-relevant topics.
- **Working groups**: On topics including AI ethics, Te Tiriti and AI, AI in healthcare, and AI safety (more recently).

## Funding and influence

As an industry-led body, AI Forum NZ wields convening rather than regulatory power. Its influence operates through:

- Authoritative-seeming public reports framing national discussion.
- Membership relationships with senior government officials.
- Convening power — events bring together industry, agencies, and academia.

Funding model (industry membership) is itself a frequent subject of critique — see Reception.

## Reception and critique

- *Supportive view*: AI Forum NZ provides valuable infrastructure for a small country's AI ecosystem; consolidates input that would otherwise be diffuse; produces useful aggregate data on industry trends.
- *Critique — industry capture*: As an industry-funded body, the Forum's policy framings predictably favour industry interests (low regulatory friction, public investment in industry-adjacent skills). Civil-society and academic critics argue independent AI policy analysis in NZ is under-resourced as a result.
- *Critique — Tiriti engagement*: Te Tiriti-grounded AI governance work has grown in NZ (Te Mana Raraunga, individual Māori scholars) largely outside the AI Forum, with the Forum engaging only intermittently with Māori-led frameworks.
- *Critique — safety focus*: The Forum's traditional emphasis on economic opportunity has integrated AI-safety framings slowly compared to UK / EU equivalents.

## Relationship to government

- The NZ Algorithm Charter ([[NZ Algorithm Charter]]) was developed by Stats NZ with input from various stakeholders including AI Forum members.
- Submissions to parliamentary inquiries (e.g., the 2023 Privacy Act review).
- No statutory role; influence is consultative.
