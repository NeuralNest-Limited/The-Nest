# US AI Safety Institute (AISI)

> US federal government AI safety institute housed within the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). Established 2024 under the Biden administration's Executive Order on AI; conducts evaluations of frontier models and develops AI safety standards.

## Founding and structure

Established 2024 within NIST, following the October 2023 Biden Executive Order on AI (EO 14110). Operates as part of NIST with mandate to develop AI safety science, evaluate frontier models, and support standards development.

Subject to political uncertainty: the Trump administration's January 2025 executive actions revoked EO 14110 and reframed federal AI policy priorities. As of 2026, the US AISI's continuing operations and budget are subject to ongoing political negotiation.

## Mission and focus

Stated focus areas:

1. **Evaluation methodology development**: extending NIST's existing role in measurement science to AI evaluation.
2. **Pre-deployment access agreements**: cooperation arrangements with frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, others).
3. **Standards development**: feeding into NIST AI Risk Management Framework (originally pre-AISI, now under AISI orbit).
4. **International coordination**: participation in the International AISI Network alongside UK AISI and peers.

## Notable outputs

- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** (originally published 2023, pre-AISI; AISI continues development): widely-cited voluntary framework for organisational AI risk management.
- **Pre-deployment evaluation agreements** with Anthropic, OpenAI.
- **Methodological publications** on evaluation design and metrics.
- Joint publications with UK AISI on shared evaluation methodologies.

cites:: [[NIST AI RMF 2023]]

## Key people

Leadership has rotated with political cycles. The original director was Elizabeth Kelly (Biden administration). Post-2025 leadership and structure subject to administration policy.

## Funding and influence

Federal appropriations through NIST, subject to congressional process. Influence operates through:

- Standards-development authority — NIST standards carry substantial weight in US federal contracting and broader industry adoption.
- Coordinating role with UK AISI and the International AISI Network.
- Pre-deployment access providing capability information to government.

## Reception and critique

- *Within AI safety community*: positive; the institutional foothold within NIST is treated as valuable.
- *Political uncertainty*: the 2025 EO 14110 revocation created concern about continuity; the AISI's specific authorities and budget remained subject to negotiation.
- *From civil society*: mixed; concerns about institutional weakness and political vulnerability.
- *From industry*: cooperative posture but voluntary nature limits compliance demands.
- *Comparison to UK AISI*: US AISI has been judged less operationally agile due to NIST's institutional structure, though with broader standards-development reach.
