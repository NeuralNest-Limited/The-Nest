# Machine Intelligence Research Institute

> The longest-standing nonprofit dedicated to AI existential risk and alignment research. Founded as the Singularity Institute in 2000; renamed MIRI in 2013; pivoted toward policy advocacy in 2024 after publicly stating its alignment research strategy had failed to produce sufficient results.

## Founding and structure

Founded in 2000 by Eliezer Yudkowsky and Brian Atkins as the Singularity Institute for Artificial Intelligence. Renamed Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) in 2013. Operates as a US 501(c)(3) nonprofit, funded primarily by individual donations and aligned philanthropy.

## Mission and focus

MIRI's founding mission was to **ensure that the creation of smarter-than-human intelligence has a positive impact**. For roughly two decades, this took the form of mathematical research into agent foundations: decision theory, logical uncertainty, embedded agency, and the formal underpinnings of aligned superintelligence.

In 2024, MIRI publicly announced a strategic pivot. Its leadership concluded that the technical alignment problem was unlikely to be solved before transformative AI was built, and shifted resources toward advocacy for international coordination to slow or halt frontier development — including direct policy work and public communication.

## Notable outputs

- **Yudkowsky writings**: Foundational essays on alignment risk, embedded in *The Sequences*, *Inadequate Equilibria* (2017), and ongoing LessWrong posts.
- **Mathematical alignment research**: Papers on decision theory (Updateless Decision Theory, Functional Decision Theory), logical uncertainty, embedded agency, Vingean reflection. Quality of formalism praised; clear path to practical alignment less so.
- **AI Foom debate** (2008): Robin Hanson vs. Yudkowsky on whether AI capabilities would grow gradually or recursively/quickly — a foundational document in x-risk discourse.
- **Public advocacy (2023–present)**: Yudkowsky's TIME op-ed (March 2023) calling for an international moratorium with enforcement; subsequent congressional testimony and media engagement.

## Funding and influence

MIRI's influence on AI safety thought is disproportionate to its size. Many current alignment researchers (including some at Anthropic, OpenAI, and Redwood Research) cite MIRI's framing and Yudkowsky's writing as formative. Its 2024 pivot represents the most prominent organizational statement that technical alignment cannot be relied upon as the primary defense.

## Reception and critique

- *Within the rationalist / LessWrong community*: Regarded as foundational; Yudkowsky's writing constitutes a major share of the community's canonical texts.
- *Within mainstream AI*: Mixed. Some researchers credit MIRI with raising serious problems early; others view its framing as overly speculative and its math as detached from deployed systems.
- *Critiques from FAccT and civil society*: That MIRI's longtermist framing crowds out attention to near-term, current AI harms (bias, surveillance, labor displacement).
- *Internal split*: MIRI's 2024 pivot was itself contested; some former researchers argued the technical work was succeeding and should continue.
